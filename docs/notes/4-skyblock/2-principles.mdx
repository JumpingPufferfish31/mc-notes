# Principles

:::info[[All-From-One](#all-from-one)]

*Every block, items, and mobs should be **renewably obtainable** from the initial condition.*

:::

:::info[[Grinding ≠ Progression](#grinding--progression)]

*Progression should not be locked exclusively behind excessive time-gate, randomness, or grinding.*

:::

:::info[[Exploitation ≠ Technicality](#exploitation--technicality)]

*Progression should not exclusively rely on bugs, exploits, or implementation/coincidental details.*

:::

:::info[[Vanilla Game ≠ Vanilla Design](#vanilla-game--vanilla-design)]

*Progression should adhere to the vanilla design, and not necessarily the vanilla game.*

:::

## All-From-One

*Every block, items, and mobs should be **renewably obtainable** from the initial condition.*

**Obtainable** here means they can be obtained without needing 1 first.  
***Renewably* obtainable** here means they are obtainable *and* there are no limits to how many of them can be obtained.  
  For example, the [sentry armour trim in carpet sky](https://github.com/jsorrell/CarpetSkyAdditions/blob/39aa4350679036ec2a51a5c38afab5c7ea09fb68/docs/en_us/datapack.md#seeing-patterns-everywhere) is:
    * *renewable* via the usual duplicating recipe,  
    * *obtainable* by completing a challenge achievement for obtaining all banner patterns, but
    * **not** *renewably obtainable* because the obtaining method can only be completed once.  
      I.e. it is both obtainable and renewable, but if we remove it all, we cannot obtain a new one again, so it's not *renewably* obtainable.  

At first, I was thinking of just having 1 full block type as the initial condition (could still be a small island with a handful of that 1 full block).  
The block also does not necessarily have to be grass block, provided the grass block is made renewably obtainable via other mechanics.  

But now I might relax this to the starting island plus a tree instead because I still like that classical skyblock aesthetics.  
So just like carpet sky and sky void, but with the stricter condition than some of the carpet sky's mechanics.  

## Grinding ≠ Progression

*Progression should not be locked exclusively behind excessive time-gate, randomness, or grinding.*

In one extreme, we have the one block setup in snow biome trying to get a shovel from a zombie by dying before falling far enough to despawn them so we can respawn and somehow manoeuvre to kill it on that one block.  
In the other extreme, we have a standard minecraft world with plentiful resources.  

Some grinding is fine, but there should be some reasonable sub-progressions.  
E.g. gradually accumulating iron ingots from zombies to have enough for more cauldron for faster first water.  

Some randomness is fine, but there should be some way to speed-up or mitigate it.  
E.g. having a mechanic to increase the chance of lightning storm, the chance of obtaining specific enchanted book trades, the chance of spawning wandering traders.  

## Exploitation ≠ Technicality

*Progression should not exclusively rely on bugs, exploits, or implementation/coincidental details.*

**Bugs** here also include ones that are marked as features or work as intended.  
I'm fine with their use, and personally think that it is these bugs and quirks that collectively defines the charm of minecraft.  
But the main point is that relying on them shouldn't be the *only* way to make progress.  

**Implementation details** here means the exact details in the code that implements the imprecise design behind them.  
**Coincidental details** here means the side effects and emergent details in the code that may not necessarily have any design behind them.  

## Vanilla Game ≠ Vanilla Design

*Progression should adhere to the vanilla design, and not necessarily the vanilla game.*

**Vanilla design** here means sticking to the core design directions and intentions behind the game (e.g. gnembon).  
**Vanilla game** here means the base, unmodified base game (e.g. Ethoslab and TMC).  

For example, there's an extra spawning logic for strays that *looks like* it's trying to move the skylight check at the spawning spot to above the [top-most connected powder snow](https://mcsrc.dev/2/21w20a/net/minecraft/world/entity/monster/Stray#L29-34).  
Except that it starts looking from the block above the spawning spot, and checks the skylight *at* the top-most connected powder snow from that point, always resulting in at most 14 skylight levels (as opposed to the block on top of that powder snow).  
So the vanilla game effectively has no special spawning logic with powder snow for strays despite the new pieces of code,
and we don't know what the vanilla design behind that could've been.  
But the main point is just that there's a distinction.
